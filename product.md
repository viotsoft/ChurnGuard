## What We're Building

**ChurnGuard** is an AI-powered B2B SaaS platform that helps e-commerce shop owners predict which customers are about to stop buying — and automatically executes retention actions (discounts, win-back emails, personalized offers) with owner approval. The owner doesn't get a dashboard with scores. They get a notification: "We're about to lose Maria. Here's the action we recommend — approve?"

**One-line pitch:** Klaviyo tells you who's at risk. ChurnGuard tells you what to do and does it.

## Landscape Analysis

### Layer 1 — Tried and true
Gainsight/ChurnZero dominate B2B SaaS customer success. For ecommerce: Klaviyo Predictive Analytics (churn risk, next purchase date, LTV) is already embedded in most Shopify stores for free.

### Layer 2 — Current market (2026)
- **Existing Shopify apps:** Chupper, EigenAI Analytics
- **Platform players:** Klaviyo (free, embedded), Braze (enterprise), Attentive (SMS-first)
- **New entrants:** QuadSci ($8M raised, SaaS-focused not ecommerce), Churn Buster (subscription billing recovery)
- **Critical failure pattern:** Models fail not from bad algorithms but from dirty data, missing action plans, and offline/online performance gaps. One documented case: 85% precision in testing → 18% precision in production. Team stopped trusting the scores within 6 weeks.

### Layer 3 — Where conventional wisdom is wrong (EUREKA)
Every existing tool tells a shop owner WHO is at risk. None reliably tell them WHAT TO DO and then DO IT FOR THEM. Small e-shop owners are solo operators. They can't run manual retention campaigns. The unlock isn't better prediction scores — it's **prediction + automated execution**. An owner who gets "Customer X has 80% churn probability" and an empty inbox is in the same position as before. An owner who gets "We sent Maria a 15% discount for her birthday — she bought within 24 hours" has a retention engine.

**The gap:** Last-mile execution. ChurnGuard's moat is not the ML model — it's closing the distance between signal and action.

---

## Premise Challenge

1. **Is this the right problem?** The stated problem is "churn prediction." The real problem is "revenue lost to preventable churn that shop owners can't act on fast enough." Prediction is necessary but not sufficient. The plan must include execution, not just scoring.

2. **What's the actual outcome?** Retained customers. Higher LTV. Less CAC dilution. The metric is euros retained per month per shop, not model accuracy.

3. **What if we do nothing?** Shop owners lose 70-75% of customers per year. They pay for Klaviyo, get its basic churn features, and most don't use them. The pain is real but diffuse — nobody is on fire today.

**Agreed premises:**
- P1: Prediction without action is a dashboard, not a product
- P2: The target customer is time-poor — they need automation, not another tool to check
- P3: Klaviyo is a distribution partner and integration target, not a competitor to beat
- P4: The data integration problem (getting clean behavioral data from Shopify/WooCommerce) is harder than the ML problem
- P5: The first 10 customers are more important than the first 1,000 features

## Implementation Alternatives

### Approach A — Shopify-first MVP (score + weekly digest)
**Summary:** Shopify app that analyzes order history, assigns churn risk score, sends weekly email digest to owner with "top 10 at-risk customers."
- Effort: S (4-6 weeks human / 1-2 weeks CC+gstack)
- Risk: Low
- Pros: Fast to market, easy to explain, easy to install
- Cons: Klaviyo already does this for free. Hard to charge real money.

### Approach B — Platform + Automation Engine ✅ SELECTED
**Summary:** Multi-platform (Shopify + WooCommerce) behavioral tracking via webhooks, ML churn scoring, automated campaign execution integrated with Klaviyo/Mailchimp/SMS, owner approval flow (email/mobile notification).
- Effort: L (3-4 months human / 3-4 weeks CC+gstack)
- Risk: Medium
- Pros: Real differentiation — closes the signal-to-action gap. Integrations become switching costs. Charges $99-299/month defensibly.
- Cons: More complex integration surface. Data quality is the hard problem, not the model.

### Approach C — AI Retention Agent (autonomous, goal-based)
**Summary:** Owner sets a goal ("reduce churn by 20% this quarter") and a budget. Agent runs A/B tests, sends campaigns, adjusts messaging. Owner reviews results weekly, not individual actions.
- Effort: XL (6-12 months human / 6-8 weeks CC+gstack)
- Risk: High
- Pros: 10x more valuable than B if it works. Creates a category.
- Cons: Requires solving AI reliability + trust problems before finding PMF. High failure rate in production (see Layer 2 failure pattern).

**Recommendation: B.** Approach A gets killed by Klaviyo's free features. Approach C is the right long-term vision but wrong first product. Approach B is the right wedge — it charges real money, has a defensible moat, and builds toward C over 12-24 months.

## Security & Data Considerations

- **PII exposure:** Customer names, emails, purchase history flow through ChurnGuard. GDPR compliance is non-negotiable (EU market). Data processing agreements with each shop. Data minimization — store behavior signals, not raw PII.
- **API credential security:** Shopify/WooCommerce API tokens stored encrypted. Rotatable. Never logged.
- **LLM prompt injection:** Action recommendation engine uses customer data as input to an LLM. Customer-controlled fields (product names, review text) could contain injection attempts. Sanitize before LLM input.
- **Multi-tenancy isolation:** Shop A cannot see Shop B's data. Row-level security from day one.

### Architecture Decisions (D1–D5)

| # | Decision | Outcome |
|---|----------|---------|
| D1 | ML vs rule-based v1 | **Rule-based RFM for v1.** ML + LLM deferred to v2. No cold-start, no ML engineer needed to ship. |
